When we mapped job stories for the quarter, we found a recurring gap: Tato's outputs are helpful and based on a treasure trove of data. 
Accessing those outputs, however, required users being familiar with Tato, and jumping out of the tools they were working in, breaking their workflows.
As power users started to develop more skills and reports, we wanted all of their collegues and stakeholders to benefit from them.
These skills include weekly status reports, Monday morning prep digests, and team wins round-ups, all of which can be run predictably on a schedule or triggered by an event.

What we've built is now one of the most used features at Tato, and a foundation for much of our core functionality.

## Approach

I learned in our research process that the highest value work is strategic. 
I also learned that this type of work is often bogged down by the clerical work: 
extracting meeting outputs, moving data from one system to another, generating recurring outputs, etc.

This clerical work is important, but it's often repetitive, relatively simple, and often based on language patterns.
These qualities make it the ideal candidate for automation.

At the same time, while our most active users love the tool and get a lot of value from it, they were often copying outputs and pasting them in other tools. 
This made it hard to expose more users to the tool, and also limited the outputs to plain text that could be copied and pasted.

Ultimately I wanted to help Project Managers focus on the highest value work, and (as per my OKR) make accessing Tato so frictionless that it becomes a core part of the user's daily workflow. 
These learnings led me to generate a few job stories that became the spine of the project:

A routine is three choices: a skill or report that works well, a trigger,
and a destination. To make that first routine easier to reach for, empty projects ship with a
few ready-made templates: a weekly status report, a Monday morning
prep digest, and a team wins round-up. This makes it easier for users to get started with automations, and reduces the dreaded blank page syndrome.

### Time and event based triggers

Based on the core workflows we identified, we decided to start with two main triggers: 
schedule-based (daily, weekly, monthly) and event-based (relative to the start of a meeting, or once a meeting ends).

To make these automations more targeted and contextually aware, we also built a suite of filters, 
meaning the automation can be fired only when a meeting title includes a keyword or specific attendees, 
or even if a key stakeholder wasn't able to attend that specific meeting.

### Meeting users where they work

These outputs, however, are only valuable if they reach a wide audience. Reports, agendas, and celebrating team wins are assets prepared for a larger set of stakeholders. 
These stakeholders spend most of their day in Teams and email, rather than in Tato, so we built integrations with
both and made them first-class outputs. 

A routine can post to a Teams channel, a private Teams message, email an arbitrary list of users, and outputs to Tato's own chat.
Tato now does the work where people actually need it, and more closely aligns with our vision of Tato being a virtual teammate who helps you work better and faster.

A daily scrum agenda routine is a good example: it fires
right after the standup ends, pulls open risks and action items straight
from the project's RAID log, and posts the summary to both a Teams
channel and Tato's own chat.

### Keeping v1 small

We cut aggressively. Multi-step routines and conditional logic went to v2. The
definition of done was one sentence: a user can schedule a skill to run
weekly and the output lands in an inbox. Everything else could wait until
that worked.

## Outcome

Routines turned Tato's outputs from something you fetch into something that
arrives. A risk summary before the steering committee, a decision log at
the end of the week, without anyone remembering to ask. It also became the
base other features build on: report scheduling and output routing were
unified on top of routines soon after.

Routines were the biggest lever on an objective I own: make Tato's AI
touchpoints so frictionless that using them becomes instinctive instead of
a context switch, measured by doubling the number of times Tato gets
invoked. Every routine that runs is an invocation nobody had to remember.
