A RAID log is a collection of Risks, Action Items, Issues, and Decisions.
It documents the status of a project as it evolves, identifying threats to
the critical path and ensuring appropriate ownership and traceability.

In digital transformation programs, where projects span three to five
years, cross project dependencies, large teams, and even larger budgets
make a RAID log critical to success and extremely difficult to manage.

<RaidTypes />

RAIDs crop up in meetings, chat messages, emails, reports, and any other
surface where people interact. They get discussed and tackled in an even
broader set of tools, and their evolution is long and rarely clear-cut.
This complexity is an opportunity for Tato to put its deep project
knowledge, integrations, and agents to work to extract, monitor, and
document RAID items across the lifecycle of a multi-year project.

## Extraction and human-in-the-loop

Meetings are the primary source of RAID items, but the items themselves
are usually managed in Jira, Smartsheet, or plain old spreadsheets. Our
first job was consolidating those sources into a normalized log that could
act as a single source of truth. Tato updates the log after any activity,
and pulls from external tools periodically or on command through MCPs and
bespoke integrations.

<ExtractionFlow />

The RAID updates on the activity summary page started as a simple section
showing the under-the-hood thinking of our agent. Exposing that machinery
is almost always a mistake that overloads the user, and despite our best
efforts, LLMs produce volume, which leads to an overwhelming number of
items.

Hiding the machinery was the first improvement, and it cut a lot of noise
on its own, since multiple snippets often related to a single update on a
single item. From there I reduced the amount of text and added icons and
avatars, which improved legibility and hierarchy and made the whole list
easier to parse.

<NeedsReview />

I also exposed two primary actions on each item, so tracking or dismissing
is always within reach and the log stays tidy. Dismissing tucks an item
away, while tracking feels impactful and energetic. Tracking means an item
is exceptionally relevant, so I doubled down on that energy with a subtle interaction.

<TrackPress />

This first iteration landed well, with early feedback showing a perception
that Tato was smarter and more nuanced. We've found our users' quantitative perception to be just as important as the qualitative improvements when it comes to interacting with an LLM.

To take it further I reworked the
summary page from a single vertical column into two columns, giving RAID
management its own first class surface where the user keeps meeting
context in view while digging into items for proper grooming.

<SummaryPanel />

## An auditable paper trail

A well managed RAID log is also an accurate record of a project's
evolution, and of what the team did to mitigate critical risks. Our
history view was hard to reach, sometimes hidden behind two separate
clicks, and it focused on diffs between versions. Diffs matter, but they
fail to paint the most important picture: what caused a change, and when.

Clarity matters even more when working alongside an agent. The history
documents which changes the agent made and the reasoning behind them, so
the human can understand them and correct them when necessary.

<HistoryDrawer />

I tried many iterations for showing an item's history and ultimately kept
the drawer we already had. The drawer offers the most real estate for
understanding a risk and its history while letting the user stay in their
current workflow, moving easily between every item in a list. Making it
more visual and tightening the hierarchy also gave space back and
increased legibility.

## The ultimate control centre

The last piece of this iteration was an improved control centre. Much of
the work happens in the context of an activity summary, and the components
and flows are largely shared, but we still wanted a delightful surface for
managing the full log.

<RaidTable />

I started with basic UX improvements: tightening space for
more breathing room, grouping related controls, and making the table
contents scrollable rather than the page, so the header, footer, and every available
action stay visible. 

Along with all of the other status icons to show impact and likelihood, I built this Linear inspired fractional progress indicator so we can smoothly animate between the states, and support any number of intermediate states.

<ProgressDemo />

Finally, based on user feedback, I made the table editable without opening
an item. That feedback partly inspired the drawer redesign too, making
every property of an item visual as well as editable inline.

## The future of RAID

The goal of this project, and of Tato as a whole, is to make the processes
behind these large transformations more automated and effortless, shifting the
human workload from note taking and data transfer to verification and the
occasional correction.

This RAID log is one small step toward a future where humans and AI
collaborate to ensure successful business transformations.
