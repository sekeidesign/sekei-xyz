## Approach

Q2 became a reset. We shifted our ICP from the Systems Integrator who sets
up the ERP to the end customer who actually uses it, which meant closing a
real understanding gap. ERPs are foreign territory for most people, and the
team needed a shared sense of who we serve.

I started by aligning with sales, then ran hour-long sessions with end
customers. The sessions stayed open: no script, just understanding their
world and their pains.

### The journey map

From those conversations I built a User Journey map, which became the
foundation for the rest of our product work. Instead of scattered notes on
what different customers said, it laid out their process step by step:
what they're doing, where they get stuck, and what they need from an ERP
integration at each point. That gave sales, design, and engineering one
artifact to point to instead of separately held assumptions.

### Job stories

I broke the journey down into Job Stories, then grouped those into themes
and workflows. Job stories describe a situation and a motivation rather
than a persona and a feature request, which kept us focused on what
customers were trying to get done instead of designing around who we
assumed they were.

<Figure
	src="/casestudies/q2-reset/job-stories.webp"
	alt="Tato job stories board"
	caption="Job stories grouped into themes and workflows"
/>

### The knowledge base

Alongside it, I built an internal knowledge base, loosely modeled on
[Karpathy's LLM wiki pattern](https://gist.github.com/karpathy/442a6bf555914893e9891c11519de94f):
a living wiki the team keeps current instead of re-reading raw notes every
time a question comes up. It's vectorized with
[qmd](https://github.com/tobi/qmd) so it's efficient for LLMs to parse, and
it's become the place we check instead of re-explaining ERP concepts from
scratch. We use it to answer questions, write PRDs, and help with
diagramming in Figma.

## Key learnings

	label="Key learnings"
	items={[
		"Project Managers live on a spectrum from clerical to strategic. The highest value work is strategic, but they get bogged down in clerical work.",
		"End customers are good at their jobs, but often inexperienced in ERPs. They have knowledge gaps they don't know about, and those gaps are what cause friction with their consultants.",
		"Implementations are long and complex, and most failures trace back to early miscommunication and requirements missed at the start.",
	]}
